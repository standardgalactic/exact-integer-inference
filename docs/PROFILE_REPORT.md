# Activation Profile of SmolLM2-135M for an Integer Execution Engine

## Pins and reproducibility

The model is HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M at revision 93efa2f097d58c2a74874c7e644dbc9b0cee75a2 (Apache 2.0). The weight file is 269,060,552 bytes with SHA-256 80521b40281d6ce74e35c9282c22539e75aa0ac8578892b2a59955ef78d55da1; every tensor, the configuration, and all tokenizer assets are hashed in model_manifest.json. The checkpoint stores bf16 tensors, so widening to float32 is exact and the float baseline carries exactly the checkpointed values. Two configuration values matter for the integer specification and are easy to assume wrongly: rope_theta is 100000, not the common 10000, and rms_norm_eps is 1e-5. The held-out text is the test split of Salesforce/wikitext (wikitext-2-raw-v1) at dataset revision b08601e04326c79dfdd32d625aee71d232d685c3, file hash 5f1bea067869d04849c0f975a2b29c4ff47d867f484f5010ea5e861eab246d91. Rows are joined with blank lines, tokenized once with no BOS or EOS inserted (304,978 tokens), and cut into 595 non-overlapping windows of 512 tokens, dropping a 338-token tail. Windows 0 to 63 calibrate and windows 64 to 127 validate, so no clipping rate below was measured on data that chose a scale. Each window runs as its own sequence at batch size one.

The reference forward pass is written by hand in float32 so every site can be observed. On three windows it matches the Hugging Face eager implementation to a maximum logit difference of 8.0e-5, identical argmax at every position, and negative log-likelihood equal to about seven digits. Profiling runs on one thread with deterministic algorithms enabled. A two-window repeat reproduced the content digest of the profile bit for bit; the full 64-window profiles carry digests (calibration 716962896a3f..., validation 5596da87e20d...) but were each run once, so full-run repeatability rests on that smaller test and on the fixed settings. Float perplexity on the scored tokens is 20.22 (calibration) and 20.89 (validation), 32,704 tokens each. Held out here means held out from our calibration only; the text is not guaranteed absent from the pretraining mixture.

## What was recorded

At every layer the profiler records the residual stream at block input, after attention, and after the MLP; both RMSNorm stages including the mean square, reciprocal root, normalized value before the weight, and weighted output; Q, K and V projections before rotation and Q and K after it; attention scores, scores after subtracting the row maximum, row maxima, exponential sums, probabilities, and the probability on key position zero; the attention context and output projection; and the MLP gate, up, SiLU output, gated product, and down projection, plus the final norm and logits. Each site keeps exact signed extrema, per-channel absolute maxima and sums of squares, a per-token absolute-maximum histogram, and a histogram of absolute values with 32 linear sub-bins per octave. Because octave edges are exact powers of two, exceedance counts at power-of-two thresholds are exact, and percentiles are interpolated inside bins at most about 3 percent wide. Masked attention entries (49.9 percent of each score matrix) are never passed to any statistic; the float reference sets them to negative infinity before the softmax. Percentile, per-channel, and clipping tables are in profile_summary.csv and profile_summary.json.

## The residual stream and the sink token

The residual stream is the one place where INT16 storage with a single scale fails, and the reason is a single token. From layer 12 through layer 28, one token per window carries a value near 20,500 in channel 507, while the median absolute residual value is between 1.6 and 10.9 across those layers and the next largest channels reach only about 1,400 and 670. In all 64 calibration windows the first position is such a token; three further cases occur at positions 1 and 2 of windows that begin with repeated digit tokens. The value is written by the layer 11 MLP (gated channel 1229 reaches 2,519 and the down projection writes 20,517 into channel 507) and mostly removed by the layer 28 MLP (down projection 15,975 in the same channel), leaving 4,348 at the input of layer 29. With one spare bit over the calibration maximum, a per-tensor INT16 scale needs a step of 2.0 at every layer from 12 to 28 and the median residual value spans about 0.8 steps at layer 12, 1.6 at layer 20 and 5.4 at layer 28. Removing channel 507 from the range calculation only reaches 13 steps at the median at layer 12. Resolution is already marginal earlier: at layers 2 to 10 the per-tensor INT16 median spans only 14 to 53 steps. Two alternatives were measured on the same data. INT32 storage with 15 fractional bits covers every layer with a spare bit (largest magnitude 20,558 against a range of 65,536) and spans at least 2,258 steps at the median everywhere. INT16 with one power-of-two exponent per channel keeps every channel's root-mean-square value at 346 steps or more at every layer. Neither has been tested for model quality, which needs the integer engine.

Two consequences for the specification follow. The residual add is elementwise, so either alternative keeps it exact. But the RMSNorm statistic squares the residual, and an INT32 residual has a worst-case bound of 576 times 2^62, which exceeds INT64 at the proven-bound level even though the measured values (one channel near 4.5e17 after squaring in raw units at f = 15) fit. The squared statistic therefore needs an explicit pre-shift or saturation rule, which is the kind of information-discarding point you asked to have fixed.

## Other sites

Under the default rule, every other site stores comfortably in INT16 with a per-tensor scale: Q, K, V, attention context, gate, up, SiLU output and the norm outputs keep channel root-mean-square values at 36 steps or more at nearly every layer. The exceptions are narrow. Layers 11, 28 and 29 write massive values through the gated product (mlp_gated starves 1,534 of 1,536 channels at layer 11) and the down projection at layers 11 and 28 (568 of 576 channels starved), and the median of the gated product spans fewer than 64 steps at 26 of 30 layers. A few isolated channels fall below 8 steps in the layer 0 first norm output (3 channels), the second norm output (one channel in each of four layers, worst at layer 24), and the layer 18 attention context (15 channels). In total 118 of 876 site-layers are flagged, 27 each for the three residual sites, 26 for the gated product, 7 for the down projection, 2 each for attention context and SiLU output. The 64-step flag threshold is a screening rule I chose, not a derived bound.

## Attention

Shifted scores (the exponent input) reach 86 in magnitude, and between 32 and 79 percent of valid entries beyond layer 0 sit at 8 or lower below the row maximum, so the exponential table needs a defined floor rather than a domain that reaches 86. Probabilities are bounded by 1, but exactly 1 occurs on the first row, so an unsigned 16-bit format needs either saturation at 65535 or one extra bit. At 16 fractional bits up to 53 percent of valid probability entries at some layers round to zero, yet the mean probability mass discarded per row is at most 2.0e-4; at 12 fractional bits it reaches 4.5e-3. The probability on key position zero has a median of 0.37 at layer 12 and 0.67 at layer 20, consistent with the sink token above. Whether to skip masked positions structurally (my preference, since no sentinel value then needs defining) or to compute them and force an exact zero contribution is a specification choice for the tiled GPU kernels.

## Normalization

The mean square spans 27 octaves (0.0055 to 741,000) and its reciprocal root spans 13.5 octaves (0.00116 to 13.5) over the profile, with the upper end set by the massive token at layer 12 and the lower end by the small embedding scale at layer 0. A reciprocal-root approximation therefore needs an exponent normalization step rather than a direct table over the input, and the table's accuracy should be judged at the sink token's operating point as well as the typical one.

## Tied embedding matrix

The matrix has global maximum 1.98, root-mean-square 0.135 and no row that rounds entirely to zero under one INT8 scale. Quantizing it at INT8 while holding every other number in float, over 16 validation windows (8,176 tokens), the two roles behave very differently. With one per-tensor scale, the lookup role gives a KL divergence of 8.0e-4 against the float model and 98.8 percent top-1 agreement, while the output head gives 1.6e-2 and 90.8 percent. With one scale per vocabulary row the numbers drop to 6.4e-5 and 99.6 percent for the lookup and 8.5e-4 and 98.0 percent for the head, and using the same per-row matrix in both roles gives 9.1e-4 and 98.0 percent. A per-row scale is one vector of 49,152 values attached to one stored matrix, so the tied weight is still represented once; the head then needs a per-column rescale of each logit accumulator into a common logit format. Differences in negative log-likelihood are below 1.1e-2 per token for per-tensor and below 3e-4 for per-row, and several of the small ones change sign, so KL and top-1 agreement are the dependable indicators at this sample size. For the final normalized hidden state stored as INT16, 5 to 9 fractional bits are all benign (KL from 1.6e-4 down to 6e-7), 10 starts clipping, and 11 or more breaks the model (top-1 agreement 82 percent at 11 and 31 percent at 13). That matches the profile, where the final hidden maximum is 57.1 and the one-spare-bit rule picks 8 fractional bits.

## Proposed scaling scheme

The default rule stores each site as INT16 with one static per-tensor exponent chosen as value = q times 2 to the minus f, f = 15 minus k, k = floor(log2 of the calibration absolute maximum) plus 2. Under this rule, validation windows saturate zero values across all 876 site-layers. With no spare bit (k one smaller), a handful of sites saturate on validation (for example 8 attention-context values, 6 attention row maxima, 6 V projection values), and the largest growth of a validation maximum over its calibration maximum was 1.77 for the first norm's mean square and 1.69 for the gated product, so one spare bit is adequate here but not generous at the heavy-tailed sites; the specification needs saturating arithmetic defined regardless. The class assigned to each site-layer, its exponent, its median in steps, and its validation clip count are in scaling_proposal.json: 545 site-layers stay INT16 per tensor, 118 are flagged for the wider or per-channel alternatives above, 30 probability sites take an unsigned 16-bit format, and the normalization and exponential-sum scalars take wide fixed point.

## Limits of the evidence

Everything here is one text source with no BOS token, 512-token windows, and 64 windows for each of calibration and validation, so absolute maxima at rare tokens can still grow. Percentile and clipping figures describe this distribution, not a worst case, and proved accumulator bounds remain a separate obligation. No integer arithmetic has run yet: resolution numbers measure how many steps the median value spans, not model quality, and INT8 quantization of the linear weights has not been profiled at all. Per-channel candidates were assessed by channel root-mean-square resolution only. The embedding study used 16 windows and a single configuration for each condition.
